Dissertation

Signaling Secrets: Strategic Intelligence, Technology, and the Sources and Methods Dilemma

My dissertation explores the conditions under which intelligence collection technologies can enable leaders to disclose covertly acquired knowledge about rivals' capabilities. I argue that the answer depends on the interaction between a collection method's political characteristics and its technical vulnerability to countermeasures. When a method enjoys normative cover and is resilient to countermeasures, leaders can signal what they know without destabilizing the intelligence relationship. When these conditions are absent, disclosing intelligence risks diplomatic crises or the loss of the source, trapping leaders in a "sources and methods dilemma."

I test this theory through archival case studies of Cold War aerial, satellite, and undersea intelligence operations, analysis of contemporary AI-enhanced ISR and space-based sensing, and experimental wargames with elite national security participants.

Committee: Jack Snyder (chair), Erica Lonergan, Allison Carnegie, Elizabeth Saunders
